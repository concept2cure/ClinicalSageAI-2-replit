# D2: CSR draft control, slices 3 and 4. Revise the text shown by instruction; alternatives for a selected passage

Launch row D2, workstream W1 (Authoring). Design: `docs/design/CSR_DRAFT_HUMAN_CONTROL_2026-10-29.md`, slices 3 and 4, and its "Decisions on slice 1's questions", which bind this work. Founder direction of 2026-10-29: AnA drafts the CSR in the canvas and the editor, offers options, and the person accepts, denies or edits what it proposes, so the person stays in control.

This work **completes** the review panel (slice 1, `7b4a16cf8`) and the existing draft route, and **reaches** the existing proposal door from a selection in the editor. It adds no surface, route, table, migration, model or CSS. The draft route takes two optional request fields; it is extended, not duplicated.

Status: slice 3 and slice 4 are implemented and verified in the working tree, not committed (the lead commits). The earlier, stopped attempt is preserved in `../slice-3-4-wip/` and is superseded by this folder.

### A deviation from the design, and why

Slice 4 says each passage alternative "is a candidate with its own check". Each has its own check and its own turn record, but **none is parked as a candidate**. The only consumer of a parked candidate is the whole-section accept, which replaces the section with the candidate's content; a parked passage could therefore be accepted as the whole section. The replacement goes in as a tracked change through the proposal door, as slice 1's chosen paragraphs do, and its lineage is that path's (below). The route test pins it ("parks no candidate, so the whole-section accept can never take a passage").

## Part 11 lineage effect

- **A revision** is a new single-use candidate. The base is read and never consumed, so it still accepts. The new candidate's generator names `derivedFrom: { draftId, contentSha256, instruction }`, and the whole accept writes it to the audit row with the generator. The revision's turn record holds the instruction in its request (`revise: { fromDraftId, instruction, baseContentSha256 }`) and the base text as model input.
- **The base is the candidate as parked, never the panel's text.** Words the person typed into the panel are never sent to the model to come back credited to it; the panel says so when the shown text was edited.
- **A passage replacement** is AnA's tracked change of exactly the selection, with `data-source-record` set to that alternative's turn record, under the governed-write gate and the saved-base check. No candidate is consumed and nothing is saved. On save, AnA's unchanged words verify against the turn record and are credited with the serving model; words the person typed are theirs. The replacement carries no source citations, and the panel says so before the click (design slice 7 is the follow-up).
- Accepting is never approving. Review, the scientific-review attestation and the signature are unchanged.

## Rule 2: the model never supplies a number

- Both prompt blocks tell the model to supply no number, result or statistic that the evidence shown does not state, even when the instruction asks for one.
- The engine's check re-runs on every revision and every passage alternative. The base is not a source: a figure carried from it that no source holds is "not found". A figure the instruction asked for and the model wrote is "not found". A figure the person gave in the instruction is attributed to the person (`fromPerson`), never "found". The route test pins all three (36 months, 38%, 42%).
- Slice 2's CSR engine figure gate has not landed. Until it does, a non-engine figure in a CSR results section is caught by the answer check against the sources shown and by the saved-CSR verdict at critique and release, as decision 6 records.

## Reviews applied to the UI changed

- **Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA):** native buttons, radios, select and text input with `<label htmlFor>`; the revise controls and the passage's Replace are described by their notes (`aria-describedby`); the passage view is a labelled region whose heading takes focus on opening; arrival is a polite status and refusals are alerts. A browser drops focus from a control disabled while AnA works; when the work ends, focus that fell to the page returns to the control pressed, and focus the person moved elsewhere stays theirs (`useFocusReturn`, tested in both views). Nothing relies on colour alone: the redline is `<ins>`/`<del>`.
- **Microcopy:** every refusal names its remedy and says that nothing was drafted or changed; the notes say before the click what the act does and does not do (no source citations; AnA does not supply figures; nothing is saved or accepted). Labels are what was asked ("Revised: Shorter", "Concise"), never the model's description.
- **Motion:** none added. The wait is the existing `AnaActivity`.
- **Regulatory UX (Part 11):** no auto-save, no auto-accept; accept stays a separate act; a revision never overwrites what it revises; the record of what was asked is in the turn record and, on accept, the audit row.
