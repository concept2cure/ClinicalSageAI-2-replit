# X01 — AS2 engineering contract, 14 October 2026

Scope: replace the shared raw-body AS2 envelope with CMS/S-MIME signing and recipient encryption, verify agency MDNs, and retain tenant-bound receipt/acknowledgement evidence through the canonical FDA gateway. The gateway registry/accounts/status/UI work is owned by the separate gateway workstream.

The implementation uses the existing system OpenSSL CMS engine, invoked with argument arrays and private temporary files. Runtime availability is a named dependency and is reported honestly. No production package dependency is added. Sponsor credentials remain in the existing account/secret resolution path; keys, passwords and bundle bodies are never logged or returned by capability endpoints.

The signed MIME entity retains the exact payload bytes. A detached CMS signature over that entity is enclosed in a recipient-encrypted CMS envelope. Acceptance requires a cryptographically verified, pinned agency-signed MDN tied to the original AS2 Message-ID and the recorded MIC. An unsigned, altered, wrong-signer, wrong-message or wrong-MIC receipt cannot establish delivery. An unconfirmed response after bytes may have left the process retains the existing ambiguous-delivery posture and cannot authorize a duplicate send.

Acknowledgements are stored as received, with their actual source and binding, and may advance only the matching organization's recorded transmittal. The existing distinction between platform-generated transmittal records and agency-issued acknowledgements remains visible. Engineering fixtures are local roundtrips, tamper/refusal cases and tenant boundaries; no agency endpoint is contacted.

Engineering completion does not establish operational qualification. FDA UAT, production account registration and agency acceptance remain explicitly unverified until actual evidence is supplied. REST cannot be called verified merely because credentials exist.

Implemented: the FDA eCTD/eSTAR and ICSR AS2 callers now use the same detached CMS SignedData MIME envelope, encrypted to the pinned recipient with AES-256-CBC. The signed entity preserves the binary payload and its MIME headers; SHA-256 MIC comparison covers those exact bytes. CMS verification selects only the pinned agency signer, ignoring embedded alternative certificates. Agency signing/encryption certificates and TLS trust anchors are separate configurations. Temporary key/content files have restricted permissions and are removed in a finally block; OpenSSL diagnostics never enter application errors. No new production package dependency was introduced.

The current official FDA AS2 User Guide v2.2 (July 2026), obtained read-only from `https://www.fda.gov/media/193542/download?attachment`, section 2.1 lists AES-128/192/256 as supported and AES-256-GCM as unsupported. Its SHA-256 file identity is `fa6658702047084d768d9ef769c94261ccad56c78eb7bf1017ffff418d139600`. This supports the selected CBC encryption algorithm; it does not establish sponsor registration, MIC negotiation or a successful agency roundtrip. Section 2.5 names MDN and center acknowledgements but provides no center ACK schema or message-binding contract. This work imports cryptographically verified AS2 MDNs only; it does not invent center ACK2/ACK3 parsing or acceptance.

An accepted MDN must verify its agency signature, original Message-ID and retained MIC. A signed negative MDN tied to that original message can record refusal without a MIC when the recipient could not decrypt the payload. Unsigned, altered, wrong-signer/message/MIC responses retain ambiguous delivery. Original public AS2 message/MIC/environment evidence is persisted before POST; its failure carries explicit proof that nothing was transmitted. Possible delivery is captured immediately after the request returns, before any post-send database write. Receipt persistence failures, including a missing row, retain `in_transit` and the duplicate-send lock. A verified refusal can release that lock only after its evidence is durable.

The reachable multipart receipt endpoint retains exact original MIME content type and receipt bytes, verifies against the transmittal's recorded environment, rechecks current organization membership/write authority, and records receipt, transmittal state and audit evidence in one transaction. It derives no tenant, actor, selected ACK stage or disposition from upload fields. Unknown legacy receipt times remain null; platform-generated downloads expose a separate generation time.

Receipt ingress also enforces an explicit filename allowlist, bounded format recognition and the canonical antivirus gate before cryptographic verification. Binary admission is limited to complete DER CMS SignedData, while detached MIME framing is text; no generic binary bypass or platform-generated scan exemption is used. Production keeps the repository's existing scanner-unavailable 503 policy. These ingress checks do not replace the pinned signer/message/MIC verification.

Sequence reconciliation uses an internal server-generated binding containing organization, sequence, bundle SHA-256, governed signature action and a fresh attempt UUID. Public metadata cannot supply that binding. Receipt reconciliation locks the same sequence, records the canonical `ECTD_TRANSMITTED` one-use signature ledger event, and changes only the exact in-flight attempt. The canonical claim acquires the sequence lock and rereads the spend ledger in a fresh statement, closing a stale-verdict race. Re-encoded older receipts cannot resolve a newer attempt even when its signature action is the same. Legacy or mismatched bindings remain explicitly `unreconciled`; no sequence is guessed by its displayed number. Receipt acceptance records transport receipt, never center acknowledgement.

Regression evidence: actual OpenSSL/pinned-certificate and in-process PostgreSQL tests reproduced both missing signature settlement and a re-encoded old MDN resolving a newer same-signature claim before the fix (2 failures/8 passes in `as2-receipt-spend-before.log`). After the fix the receipt suite passes 10 tests, including exact retained bytes, authenticated refusal, same-signature/new-attempt replay isolation, foreign tenant, viewer downgrade, wrong message/MIC, legacy binding refusal and rollback of receipt/sequence/signature-ledger changes on audit failure. An independent local mTLS peer decrypts and verifies the actual CMS envelope before signing its MDN; that suite covers successful delivery, post-send SQL throw/zero-row retention, and prewire SQL refusal. No agency endpoint was contacted. Existing network/unit regression doubles and their exact limitations are documented in `transport-socket-fixture-contract.md`.

Final consolidated offline verification passes **234/234 tests across ten suites**, including actual OpenSSL envelope/receipt checks, local mTLS delivery and post-send persistence failures, PostgreSQL receipt/ICSR claim transactions, parser cases, upload/API checks, gateway qualification and signature/attempt guard cases. The current claim suite has 13 passing tests; the earlier file named `transmit-attempt-final.log` contained a stale error-wording assertion and is not passing evidence. The corrected assertion retains the no-claim and no-wire requirements. See [transport-offline-final-verification.md](transport-offline-final-verification.md) for precise fixture boundaries and the final log.

Final review also reproduced a recovery gap: a synchronous verified agency refusal could leave a rejected transmittal beside a still-claimed sequence, and the receipt upload refused that already-rejected record. A matching independently verified negative MDN can now atomically settle the exact attempt and consume its original signature. A conflicting positive receipt remains refused. This regression failed before the remedy and passes afterward; retries require a new signature.

These fixtures establish engineering behavior only. Agency/account UAT, REST wire contracts, center ACK ingestion and production operational qualification remain unverified; gateway status and UI continue to expose `qualified: false`.
