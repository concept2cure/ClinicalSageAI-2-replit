Two design errors of mine, each caught by a test rather than by reasoning
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. comparedNothing hand-set in five detectors. A print of the real scan output showed one of the
   five assignments was never reached:

   coverage [ ...,
     {"detector":"status_conflict","read":0,"readLimit":200,"readTruncated":false,"skipped":0,
      "recorded":0,"recordTruncated":false,"comparedNothing":false}, ... ]

   read 0 and comparedNothing false. It is now derived in one place, and the cross-artifact
   detector's early return routed through the same helper so the derivation cannot be bypassed.

2. `found` used for both "pairs compared" and "issues found":

 × the detector says what it compared and what it dropped > a complete scan of two agreeing sections is complete, and reports no conflict
   → expected true to be falsy

   Two sections that were compared and AGREED (0 issues found) were reported as having compared
   nothing. `compared` and `found` are now separate fields.
