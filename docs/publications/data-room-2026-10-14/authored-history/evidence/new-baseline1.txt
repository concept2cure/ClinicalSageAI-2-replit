1. The thesis
The document is not the artifact. It is a view over a structured product record. Every tool in this market — Word, Veeva Vault RIM, MasterControl, Greenlight Guru, Rimsys — treats the document as the unit of truth. Text is typed into it. The same fact is retyped across the 510(k) Summary, the Device Description, the Indications for Use, the IFU, the labeling, the technical file, the risk file and the Declaration of Conformity. Then it drifts, and humans hunt for the drift.
The advanced move is to invert that. Facts live once, in the product record. Documents bind to them. The substantial-equivalence comparison is not authored — it is generated from the device profile and the predicate record, and stays live. Indications for Use is a single governed object that renders into Form FDA 3881, §5.5 of the 510(k) Summary, the IFU and the labeling. Change it once and every binding is flagged with an impact report before anything is filed.
That is the difference between a very good editor and a category-defining one. Word will never do it, because Word has no product record. RIM suites do not do it, because their document layer and their registration data model were built by different teams a decade apart.
1.1 Why Word-parity is the wrong target
Chasing Word feature-for-feature is a race that cannot be won and would not matter if it were. Microsoft has thirty years of head start on comment threading and equation editors. Meanwhile the things a device regulatory writer actually loses sleep over are things Word cannot help with at all:
* Did the accuracy claim in the SE table match the accuracy in the performance test report, the IFU and the labeling?
* Is the guidance document cited in the software section still current?
* Does every claim in the submission have evidence behind it that actually exists in the record?
* If the predicate is superseded, which of my seventeen documents are now wrong?
* Which of the twenty required eSTAR sections are genuinely complete, and which merely have text in them?
None of those are formatting problems. All of them are solvable if the content is structured and bound. That is where the ambition should go.
1.2 What must be true for this to work
One technical decision gates everything: content must be stored as a typed structure, not as a text blob. A plain string can be spell-checked. A typed block graph can be validated, bound, transcluded, diffed semantically, rendered into an eSTAR AcroForm field, and reasoned about by a model under constraint. Every capability in this specification is downstream of that one choice.
This also solves a current blocker outright. The 510(k) workbench cannot populate the eSTAR AcroForm field map today. With typed content, field mapping becomes mechanical rather than a manual enumeration exercise.
2. The eight layers
Each layer depends on the one below it. The ordering is not a preference; it is a dependency chain.
Layer 1 — Structured content
A block-based document schema (ProseMirror, TipTap or Lexical class) in place of the current textarea. Every block is typed and validated. The stored artifact is a document graph, not a string.
Unlocks: everything above. Also makes XML export meaningful and eSTAR field mapping tractable.
Layer 2 — Regulatory-native blocks
Not generic tables and paragraphs, but block types that know what they are. This is where the product stops resembling a word processor. See §3 for the full library.
Unlocks: validation, generation and binding, because the system understands the semantics of what is on the page.
Layer 3 — Binding and single-sourcing
Blocks bind to records — the device profile, the predicate, a test result, a hazard, a standard, a design input. A bound block is a live projection with an explicit version pin, not a copy. Changing the source produces an impact report listing every document, section and claim affected.
Unlocks: the elimination of drift as a class of problem, rather than its detection after the fact.
Layer 4 — Live compliance intelligence
Checks that run while writing, in the margin, not as a module the user must remember to visit:
* Completeness — which required sections for this filing type are missing or thin, against the full eSTAR set.
* Claim without evidence — an assertion with no bound test result, study or standard behind it.
* Cross-document inconsistency — this value contradicts the same value elsewhere in the dossier.
* Regulatory currency — you have cited a superseded guidance, a withdrawn standard, or a de-recognised consensus standard.
* Predicate risk — this difference from the predicate is the kind that historically triggers an Additional Information request.
* Plain-language check — for OTC IFU content, where FDA expects lay comprehension.
Worked example. The AeroFlow package supplied for UAT cites the May 2005 software guidance and the retired "Level of Concern" construct, both superseded by the June 2023 device software guidance and its Basic/Enhanced Documentation Level. It also states a regulation number that appears mismatched to its product code. A currency check at Layer 4 catches both as they are typed — before a reviewer, a consultant or FDA does.
Layer 5 — AI that operates on structure
Because content is typed, the model can be constrained rather than merely prompted. AnA proposes structured edits — a table row, a claim with its citation, a completed conformity entry — delivered as tracked changes attributed to AnA with SourceLinks recorded. Rules become enforceable: this block type may not be authored without a bound evidence record.
This is also where the product finally keeps its own promise. "AnA proposes; you accept and save" is the right governance posture and is already stated in the UI. Today there is no Accept control. At Layer 5 it exists, and what it accepts is a structured, attributed, provenance-carrying revision.
Layer 6 — Provenance and Part 11
Already largely designed and correct: per-section revisions, hash-sealed freeze, §11.50 meaning-of-signature with Authorship / Review / Approval, and SourceLinks that store the source's content identity so a section is flagged when its source changes.
Do not rebuild this. Build the new editing surface underneath it. It is the strongest asset the current implementation has.
Layer 7 — Rendering to many outputs
One record, many projections: eSTAR AcroForm, Word for circulation, PDF for filing, XML, EUDAMED payloads, IFU artwork feeds. Because content is typed, each renderer is a mapping rather than a reformatting exercise.
Unlocks: the submittable eSTAR the workbench currently cannot produce.
Layer 8 — Collaboration
Real-time co-authoring (CRDT-backed), tracked changes, redline compare between any two revisions, comment threads anchored to blocks, role-gated review, and an external reviewer mode with comment-only rights for consultants and notified-body contacts.
Why it matters: if review cannot happen inside the system, documents get exported to Word, edited outside, and re-imported — which destroys the audit trail everything else is built to protect.

	4. The three differentiators nobody has
Most of the specification above brings the editor to industry standard. These three would put it beyond anything currently on the market.
4.1 Generated substantial-equivalence tables that stay live
The SE comparison is the intellectual core of a 510(k) and today is hand-built in Word, every time, by every company. If the device profile and the predicate record are structured, the table is generated: attributes aligned, identical rows marked, differences isolated, and each difference demanding a rationale and an evidence link before the section can be marked complete.
When the predicate record changes — a new clearance, a recall, a superseding device — every SE table bound to it is flagged. No one does this. It is also the most natural possible use of the precedent intelligence engine, once that engine is connected to the FDA registries.
4.2 Regulatory currency as a live property of the text
Guidance documents are superseded. Standards are revised and de-recognised. Regulations change — QMSR replaced the Part 820 design-control framework in February 2026, and a great deal of existing device documentation still cites 820.30.
If citations are typed objects with pinned versions, the system can tell a writer, in the margin, that the guidance they just cited was superseded and what replaced it — and can sweep an entire back catalogue for the same problem. This is a genuine and recurring pain for every device company, and it is invisible to every document tool on the market.
4.3 Claim–evidence binding enforced at authoring time
Today, claims are written and then — optionally, later, by a human or a bolt-on checker — reconciled against evidence. Inverted: a Claim block cannot reach "complete" without bound evidence, and the binding is visible while writing. Evidence that is later withdrawn or superseded flags every claim that rests on it.
This converts submission review from an archaeology exercise into a state check, and it is the single most defensible thing a regulatory platform can offer a QA organisation.

6. Architecture decisions
DECISION
	RECOMMENDATION
	RATIONALE
	Editor framework
	ProseMirror (or TipTap over it). Lexical is a defensible alternative.
	Mature custom node types, collaborative editing, strict schema validation, and a well-trodden path for domain-specific blocks.
	Content storage
	Typed document graph (JSON), versioned per section, with the rendered form derived rather than stored.
	Enables binding, semantic diff, validation and multi-target rendering. The current text blob forecloses all four.
	Binding model
	Explicit reference with a version pin plus a live/pinned mode per block.
	Regulatory documents must be reproducible as filed, while drafts should track upstream change. Both are required.
	Change propagation
	Event-driven impact analysis producing a report, never a silent rewrite.
	A governed record may not change underneath a signatory. The user decides; the system informs.
	Collaboration
	CRDT (Yjs) over the ProseMirror document.
	Real-time co-authoring without lock contention, and offline tolerance.
	Diff and redline
	Semantic diff over the block graph, rendered as familiar redline.
	Structural diff is far more accurate than text diff for tables and lists — which is most of a device dossier.
	Word round-trip
	Structured mapping to OOXML in both directions, reusing the existing template engine.
	Import the client back catalogue; export something a consultant can redline. Table and style fidelity is the acceptance test.
	eSTAR rendering
	Deterministic map from block types to AcroForm fields.
	Removes the current manual field-map blocker and makes the submittable eSTAR achievable.
	AI integration
	Constrained structured-output proposals into a tracked-change queue, never direct writes.
	Preserves the "AnA proposes; you accept" governance promise and keeps provenance intact.

	7.1 The acceptance test for each phase
1. Phase 1: a regulatory writer reproduces the AeroFlow §5.6 comparison table — 13 rows, 4 columns — inside the editor, and exports it to Word with the table intact.
2. Phase 2: creating a 510(k) project seeds a document with the full required section skeleton, numbered, citation-tagged, with completion state per section.
3. Phase 3: an external consultant reviews a draft in redline and returns it without the document ever leaving the system.
4. Phase 4: changing the predicate on the program record flags every affected SE table and produces an impact report.
5. Phase 5: typing a citation to a superseded guidance produces a margin warning naming its replacement.
8. Closing
The ambition is correct and the market position is real. There is no device-native, structurally-bound regulatory authoring environment on the market. Word has no product record. RIM suites bolted their document layer and their data model together long after both were built. A system where the substantial-equivalence table generates itself, claims cannot exist without evidence, and superseded citations announce themselves would not be a better editor — it would be a different category of tool.
The constraint is sequence, not ambition. Every advanced capability in this specification rests on one unglamorous decision: store content as a typed structure rather than a string. Make that change and the rest becomes incremental. Skip it and none of it is reachable, however much is invested in the surface.
And protect what already works. The freeze, the hash seal, the §11.50 signature model and the SourceLinks provenance concept are the strongest work in the product. The failure today is not that they were built badly — it is that they were built around a textarea. Put a real document model underneath them and the governance architecture that already exists becomes the thing that makes the advanced capabilities defensible.
