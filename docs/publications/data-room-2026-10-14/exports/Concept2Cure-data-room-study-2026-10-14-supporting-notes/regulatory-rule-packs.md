# Scoped regulatory route packs and expert qualification plan

Assessment cut-off: **14 October 2026**. Scope: biotech/pharma, biologics, cell and gene therapies, medical devices and IVDs in the USA, EU/EEA, UK, Japan, Canada and Australia. Research only. This document supplements [regulatory.md](regulatory.md); it does not establish that any repository adapter is implemented or qualified. Code evidence belongs to the separate filing-code audit.

## Decisions the product must make explicit

The route key should be `authority + territory + legal product classification + intended use + application type + lifecycle state + submission date + rule version`. Customer industry labels such as “biotech” or “diagnostics” cannot select a filing format. A sponsor's expert must confirm classification, route and obligations before automated checks are treated as applicable.

Maintain five separate pathway families: **development/study permission; marketing/conformity assessment; lifecycle change; labelling/product or establishment registration; safety/post-market reporting**. A single product can need several families simultaneously. Keep the authorisation decision, dossier transmission, registry update and safety report distinct. A submission receipt, technical acceptance and scientific approval are different states.

The following matrices are implementation targets derived from regulator sources, not exhaustive legal checklists. Exemptions, reliance routes, special access, emergency programmes and local institutional approvals require additional scoped packs before support is claimed.

## Format and transition boundaries to encode

Do not duplicate a full regulatory narrative in code; encode these as scoped, versioned records, referencing the detailed verified sources in regulatory.md:

- FDA drug eCTD4: accepted new applications from 16 September 2024; existing v3 forward compatibility/two-way remain future phases on inspected FDA page.
- EU drug eCTD4: optional new CAP MAA from 22 December 2025; July 2026 plan strongly recommends Q1 2027 and mandates Q1 2028. Existing-lifecycle and non-CAP pilots are distinct. Current EU v4 validation v1.1 effective 15 July 2026.
- Japan: official v3 transition covers approval applications through 31 March 2026; use Japanese v4 approval requirements thereafter within applicable product scope. **No inference that drug eCTD4 is the format for every clinical trial, regenerative product, device or IVD.**
- Canada: activity-specific eCTD/non-eCTD and REP; verified public v4 reference is draft consultation. Device eSTAR notice updated February 2026 still specifies limited selected participants and end-2025 submission readiness; qualification must resolve actual current availability.
- Australia: current AU M1 v3.2 mandatory from 1 November 2025 with ICH v3.2.2 M2–5; AU v4 regional document/pilot is not evidence of a general production mandate.
- FDA eSTAR: current nIVD/IVD v7.1, PreSTAR v3.1; 510(k) mandatory from 1 October 2023 and De Novo from 1 October 2025, subject to scope/exemptions. PreSTAR and specified PMA routes voluntary. XML export/import does not carry attachments; acknowledged responses retain original template baseline.
- EU EUDAMED: four specified modules mandatory from 28 May 2026; Market Surveillance is authority-facing. Clinical-investigation/performance-study and Vigilance/PMS modules are not all operationally mandatory in current verified state.
- FDA safety: scoped postmarketing drug/biologic ICSRs via ESG NextGen require E2B(R3) from 1 October 2026; commercial IND database-transmission cases from 1 April 2026. These are separate from device eMDR and other specialised product channels.

The structure below is a proposed evidence model. Each field's necessity and sufficiency must be qualified against product, development stage and jurisdiction.

| Pharma/biologic | Substance/product, batches, manufacturing sites/process, analytical methods, stability, clinical population/endpoints, analysis sets and label claims; biosimilar/reference-product comparability when applicable. | IDs/versions/units, mandatory artefact presence, schema/data conformance, dataset/Define-XML consistency, numeric agreement across reports and tables. | Study adequacy, CMC control strategy, comparability, clinical/statistical inference, benefit–risk, relevance to approved claim. |

## Route-pack template and responsibility model

Each pack should contain:

1. **Applicability decision:** regulator/territory, product legal category, claim/intended purpose, study/marketing status, route/centre, local sponsor/representative, exceptions/reliance, submission date and signed rationale.
2. **Requirement manifest:** source/version/status/effective date, deterministic predicate, expert question, necessary evidence and acceptable documented exception, expected output location.
3. **Evidence manifest:** original source hash/version and rights, dataset/report lineage, product/batch/software configuration, metadata, source-language plus reviewed translation, reviewer and evidence freeze.
4. **Content/format template:** section/question schema, scoped vocabulary, mappings, filenames/paths, lifecycle operations, regional administrative metadata and official form/template version.
5. **Checks and findings:** structural validation; numerical/result reconciliation; traceability and contradictions; route applicability; unresolved scientific/technical questions, with owner and severity.
6. **Release package:** rendered/exported artefacts, checksums, rule/model/tool/run versions, exception disposition, scientific/publishing/sponsor acceptance and a retained exact submission copy.
7. **Transmission and follow-up record:** authorised channel/account role, external submission ID and acknowledgements, accepted/not-accepted status, deficiency responses and commitments. Preparation packs may stop at export; they must not display “filed” without transmission evidence.

Responsibility allocation: local regulatory expert approves jurisdiction/route/obligation scope; clinical/statistical expert accepts study conclusions; CMC/ATMP expert accepts product-quality and comparability rationale; device/IVD expert accepts classification, performance and risk evidence; pharmacovigilance expert accepts reportability, chronology and case/periodic conclusions; publishing specialist accepts technical build; privacy/data steward accepts permitted processing/transfer; sponsor authorised signatory releases the exact package. Regulators/notified bodies determine acceptance and authorisation—platform readiness labels do not substitute for their decisions.

Qualification gates:

- **Q0 scoped draft:** sources and applicability model drafted; no compliance or ready-to-file claim.

- **Q3 official-channel tested:** only where the regulator offers/authorises a sample, pilot or sandbox and all onboarding prerequisites are met; acknowledgements retained. Lack of a public sandbox is not permission to test in production.
