## What is already competitive baseline

The public market now includes AI chat over scientific/regulatory records, classification/extraction, cited report preparation, health authority response drafts, agent tools/MCP, metadata curation, regulatory change monitoring, impact assessments and AI evaluation/lifecycle management. Product strategy should treat these as useful foundations, and test their quality against client workflows, rather than label them unprecedented.

## Three differentiation hypotheses to test

These are product and architecture hypotheses, not verified claims of competitive exclusivity. Validate them in scripted vendor demonstrations and customer pilots before claiming novelty.

1. **Reproducible evidence supporting every filing claim.** Connect raw source version, dataset slice, transformation code/environment, computed result, table/figure, narrative claim, reviewer decision and final agency package in a versioned graph. Freeze a review snapshot so an auditor can reproduce the exact result after upstream data or models change. Ground AI in authorized graph nodes and show the supporting calculation or source passage. This goes beyond a citation to a notebook or PDF: the customer gains a reusable proof of how the conclusion was obtained. Moat: approved scientific-to-regulatory mappings, validated transformation recipes, correction history and trust earned through successful review, all under client ownership. Measure percentage of material claims with valid lineage, time to answer audit questions and unsupported-claim rate. Benchling cited preparation and Alation evaluation are real overlap; the test is whether end-to-end reproducibility improves materially on them.

2. **Versioned, executable filing coverage across product types and jurisdictions.** Maintain authority, product type, application type, effective date, source authority, structured requirement, approval and test fixtures. Produce evidence-backed coverage findings with explicit unknowns for unsupported combinations. Connect updates to the particular evidence and package elements affected; stage a proposed remediation and generate a reviewable difference rather than silently overwrite approved data. ArisGlobal already markets guideline-to-rule checklists and compliance agents; differentiation must be in independently verifiable tests, semantic evidence links, accurate scope and version replay, plus measurable reduction in missed requirements. Begin with a few high-value drug and device pathways; do not market universal global coverage before it is validated.

3. **A permissioned learning loop around review and filing outcomes.** Capture reviewer correction, technical validation failure, agency question, linked root cause, resolution and recurrence as structured internal evidence. Recommend prevention at ingestion, transformation and authoring time, not just at publishing. Provide organization-specific improvements first. Cross-client statistics/templates require explicit client permission, minimum cohort controls and rigorous de-identification; never share proprietary content as training data by default. Moat: longitudinal workflow-quality evidence and expert-reviewed corrective patterns that make future filings better. Measure repeated-defect rate, reviewer acceptance, cycle time and true/false findings. Avoid claiming predicted agency approval: agencies and evidence differ, and success labels are confounded.

## Due diligence questions for demonstrations and customer pilots

- Can the system reproduce a submitted table and claim from the exact frozen raw data, transformation and software versions?
- How does an upstream data change identify only the affected claims, regional dossiers and pending commitments?
- Which jurisdictions/product/application types and effective versions are actually supported and tested? Which are advisory, preview or unsupported?
- Can an agent propose actions without acting outside user/tenant permissions, and can a reviewer see/reject an exact difference?
- Are findings linked to authoritative requirement passages and auditable evidence, and is uncertainty calibrated against expert-labeled cases?
- How are model upgrades, prompt changes and retrieval changes validated against approved client-specific test sets?
- How are technical publishing validations kept distinct from substantive scientific/regulatory assessment?

## Regulatory ecosystem reminder

The MHRA's June 2025 docuBridge guidance says missing historical sequences can block validation and the agency cannot repair the sponsor's dossier. This supports treating submission history and sequence integrity as first-class data, not merely storing the latest PDFs. It does not imply all jurisdictions use the same rules or that technical validation guarantees approval. [MHRA guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/electronic-common-technical-document-ectd-submissions-update).

### Diagnostics depth: open comparison questions, not declared competitor gaps

These scenarios would test whether our combined data room/catalog/management platform offers meaningful improvements. The researched public pages do not provide enough detail to answer them consistently for competitors, and the same tests must be applied to our own product.

1. Model an IVD family by intended purpose, analyte, specimen, population, assay/software version, instrument/reagent compatibility and jurisdiction; identify precisely which product variants a proposed performance claim covers.
2. Reproduce a sensitivity/specificity or other validated performance result from the approved data slice, exclusions, reference method and computation version. Link that result to narrative claims and supporting source records while retaining later corrections.
3. Connect a change in assay, reagent, cutoff, instrument, software or intended purpose to affected risks, verification evidence, performance claims, labeling and jurisdiction-specific dossiers. Show proposed changes and reviewers before modifying approved records.
4. Preserve distinctions between scientific validity, analytical performance and clinical performance; support expert review of evidence sufficiency and applicability rather than merely checking that a document exists.
5. Carry the approved evidence into different filing formats without silently broadening claims. Show supported product/application/jurisdiction/effective-date combinations and explicit unknowns for uncovered ones.

### Revised moat language after the device/IVD review

Do not claim that structured facts, knowledge graphs, traceability, suggested design links, requirements verifiability, source-cited AI, change propagation or cross-market reuse are new by themselves. Competitor primary sources already describe those mechanisms. A credible hypothesis is **the measured quality of the combined chain**: permissions-aware evidence ingestion, expert-approved semantic relationships, reproducible computation, versioned product/claim scope, validated authority rules and filing history, connected across drug/device/IVD workflows and existing client systems.

That combination needs a narrowly scoped pilot, not an unsupported declaration of uniqueness. A useful first benchmark would compare defect recall/false positives, source correctness, time to reproduce a material claim, review effort, change-impact completeness and cross-market reuse errors against an expert-approved set of drug, device and IVD examples. Choose error thresholds by consequence; a single aggregate “90% AI accuracy” is insufficient for critical filing decisions.

### Evidence quality and limitations

The Weave/Takeda preprint evaluates two INDs and nonclinical written summaries 2.6.2/2.6.4/2.6.6. It records AI drafting times of 3.7 and 2.6 hours and compares them with an approximately 100-hour manual benchmark estimated by experienced writers. The paper reports quality scores of 69.6% and 77.9%, no detected critical regulatory errors, and remaining weaknesses in emphasis, concision and clarity. This supports a narrow first-draft productivity result, not universal filing accuracy or elimination of expert review. The vendor's two-page commentary reports different aggregate quality scores; use the study methodology and paper values when citing research rather than blend metrics. It is a preprint co-authored with vendor researchers. [Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09738), [vendor commentary](https://23328296.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/23328296/Website%20Files/Weave_Time%20and%20Quality%20Study%20Results_Sept%202025_Final.pdf).

The September 2026 announcement describes a collaboration to deploy Weave across 14 active Takeda programs and reports evaluated HAQ deployment results. Preserve that distinction: intent to expand to 14 programs does not establish complete rollout of every module in every program. [Joint announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260901015612/en/Weave-Bio-Announces-Collaboration-with-Takeda-to-Deploy-AI-Native-Regulatory-Automation-Across-Key-Drug-Development-Activities).

### Practical implications for our moat

Weave is already publicly connecting the data room, source metadata, dossier sections, sentences, verification, collaborative review and agency questions. A data-room chatbot or a generic “data-to-dossier” claim will not establish superiority. Test whether our platform can outperform at **reproducing computations and exact evidence versions**, **typed scientific/device/IVD claim applicability**, **validated requirement execution with explicit jurisdiction/version coverage**, and **change propagation into actual package and filing history**. Treat these as hypotheses requiring comparison, not claims Weave lacks them.

Historical internal documents should label external absences as “not verified in the source review” and attach review dates/URLs; internal “implemented” assertions should separately link actual routes, services, persistence, permissions and end-to-end validation. Competitive marketing cannot substitute for verification of our own production boundary.
